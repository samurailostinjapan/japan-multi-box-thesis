# Reading Guide

<p class="page-deck">The monograph is 69,291 words across 11 chapters. It can be read front to back or entered through one of three analytical movements.</p>

## I. Explain the stability

Chapters 1–3 establish why Japan's record cannot be reduced to either an overdue collapse or proof that constraints do not matter.

| Chapter | Central question | Length |
|---|---|---:|
| [**1. The Japanese Stability Puzzle**](chapters/chapter-01-introduction-japanese-stability-puzzle.md) | What changed if the state remains fully operational? | 3,578 words |
| [**2. Literature, Analytical Framework, and Method**](chapters/chapter-02-literature-framework-method.md) | How can interaction, evidence, and falsification be measured? | 4,875 words |
| [**3. Construction and Exhaustion of the Old Stability Regime**](chapters/chapter-03-construction-exhaustion-old-stability-regime.md) | Which weaknesses became stabilizers, and why is that alignment eroding? | 5,206 words |

**Movement finding:** Japan consumed the conditions that protected it. The return of inflation forced an exit from the low-rate regime before fiscal repair was complete.

## II. Trace the transmission

Chapters 4–8 follow the pressure through budgets, markets, firms, households, demography, and politics.

| Chapter | Central question | Length |
|---|---|---:|
| [**4. Fiscal Dominance and the Refinancing Constraint**](chapters/chapter-04-fiscal-dominance-and-refinancing-constraint.md) | How do higher marginal yields become a rising annual fiscal flow? | 5,143 words |
| [**5. The Bank of Japan, the JGB Market, and the Yen**](chapters/chapter-05-bank-of-japan-jgb-market-yen.md) | Why can necessary monetary action remain insufficient and internally costly? | 6,229 words |
| [**6. Domestic Transmission: Households, Firms, SMEs, and Banks**](chapters/chapter-06-domestic-transmission-households-firms-smes-banks.md) | Which balance sheets absorb the cost of aggregate stability? | 7,071 words |
| [**7. Demographic Compression**](chapters/chapter-07-demographic-compression.md) | How does population decline remove the margin for repeated deferral? | 7,742 words |
| [**8. Political Management and the Subsidy State**](chapters/chapter-08-political-management-and-subsidy-state.md) | Why does compensation preserve stability while consuming future policy room? | 7,367 words |

**Movement finding:** the domestic adjustment is not universal failure. It is the widening separation between sectors able to reprice and those forced to absorb.

## III. Test the regime change

Chapters 9–11 integrate the evidence, define conditional paths, and state what would disprove the thesis.

| Chapter | Central question | Length |
|---|---|---:|
| [**9. The Multi-Box Compression Mechanism**](chapters/chapter-09-multi-box-compression-mechanism.md) | When do stabilizers cease to be independent solutions and become stress transfers? | 7,747 words |
| [**10. Scenarios, Warning Signals, and Falsification**](chapters/chapter-10-scenarios-warning-signals-falsification.md) | Which joint signals separate managed compression from market enforcement or stabilization? | 8,580 words |
| [**11. Conclusion: The Erosion of Policy Sovereignty**](chapters/chapter-11-conclusion-erosion-policy-sovereignty.md) | What practical policy sovereignty remains when every instrument activates another constraint? | 5,753 words |

**Movement finding:** policy sovereignty erodes before financing capacity. Markets gain influence through the price and sequencing of action, not by seizing formal control.

## Editorial conventions

- Japanese fiscal years are written as FY2026, FY2027, and so forth.
- “Debt service” means budgeted interest payments plus redemption transfers unless the text explicitly says “interest expenditure.”
- “Gross issuance” includes refunding bonds and is not interchangeable with new borrowing.
- Forecasts, stress paths, and monitoring zones are labeled; they are not presented as observed facts or official targets.
- “Slow compression” describes narrowing policy freedom and rising distributional cost. It does not mean imminent insolvency.

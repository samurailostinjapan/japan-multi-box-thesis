  <div class="section-label">The thesis in one sentence</div>
  <p>Japan retains formidable financing and administrative capacity, but its fiscal, monetary, market, household, corporate, demographic, energy, and political constraints increasingly prevent any one policy tool from working without intensifying pressure somewhere else.</p>

## The object of study is the narrowing corridor

The question is not whether Japan can continue to borrow, intervene, purchase bonds, or protect households. It can. The question is how many of those objectives can remain compatible at the same time.

<div class="corridor-grid">
  <article>
    <span>Monetary</span>
    <h3>Enough restraint</h3>
    <p>Contain inflation and defend credibility—without destabilizing debt, credit, housing, and regional banks.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Fiscal</span>
    <h3>Enough relief</h3>
    <p>Protect household demand and firms—without converting every shock into a permanent public claim.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Currency</span>
    <h3>Enough yen weakness</h3>
    <p>Support overseas income and nominal revenue—without making food, energy, and imported inputs politically intolerable.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Markets</span>
    <h3>Enough price discovery</h3>
    <p>Restore a functioning JGB market—without exposing the full refinancing cost of the debt stock at once.</p>
  </article>
</div>

## How pressure moves

Slow compression is a repeated sequence, not a date on a calendar.

<ol class="pressure-chain">
  <li><strong>Control one price</strong><span>Authorities stabilize rates, yields, bills, credit, or the currency.</span></li>
  <li><strong>Open a pressure valve</strong><span>Another price or balance sheet absorbs the adjustment.</span></li>
  <li><strong>Compensate the exposed sector</strong><span>Subsidies, transfers, credit, savings, or reserves carry the loss.</span></li>
  <li><strong>Create a solution collision</strong><span>The remedy worsens a constraint already near its tolerance.</span></li>
  <li><strong>Narrow the next choice</strong><span>The original instrument remains usable, but on worse terms.</span></li>
</ol>

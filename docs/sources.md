## Evidence hierarchy

1. Bank of Japan, Ministry of Finance, Cabinet Office, Statistics Bureau, and other Japanese government releases
2. National Institute of Population and Social Security Research, GPIF, and public audit material
3. IMF, OECD, BIS, World Bank, and other multilateral work
4. Peer-reviewed and central-bank research
5. Reuters, Jiji Press, Nikkei, Kyodo, and other current reporting
6. Clearly labeled analytical calculations and mechanism-based inference

Primary releases are preferred when they establish the number or policy action. Current reporting is used for market moves, interviews, contemporaneous interpretation, and events for which the full official record was not available by the cutoff.

## Three claim classes

<div class="claim-grid">
  <article>
    <span>Observed fact</span>

  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Mechanism-based inference</span>
    <p>A causal interpretation derived from several observed facts, with its assumptions and competing explanations stated.</p>

## Definition discipline

- The initial budget is not treated as the full annual fiscal stance when a material supplementary budget is enacted.
- National debt service, interest-only expenditure, redemption transfers, and gross JGB issuance are not merged without explaining their definitions.
- A debt-service sensitivity is not presented as the Ministry of Finance baseline.
- Nominal tax-revenue growth is separated from improvement in real household purchasing power.
- A successful auction is evaluated through price, tail, concession, maturity, and repeat behavior—not coverage alone.
- BOJ estimates of the neutral rate are ranges. The monograph's 1.25–1.5 percent practical compression zone is an analytical hypothesis, not an official ceiling.
- Foreign reserves, public pension assets, corporate overseas assets, and the general budget are not treated as one interchangeable national account.

## Cutoff and versioning

The empirical record closes on **July 31, 2026**. This boundary is substantive: it prevents later developments from being written backward into forecasts and interpretations made before they occurred.

Events after the cutoff can validate, weaken, or redirect the framework. They belong in a dated revision or post-cutoff update with a clear delta against the July manuscript. They are not silently inserted into the original evidentiary record.

The framework should be revised when joint evidence changes—not when one market produces a dramatic session. The burden of proof for decompression is several years of mutually reinforcing improvement without an equivalent liability appearing elsewhere.

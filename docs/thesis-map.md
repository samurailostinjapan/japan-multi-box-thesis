# Framework Map

<p class="page-deck">Multi-box compression tracks where pressure moves after a policy succeeds at its immediate target.</p>

## A shrinking feasible set

Compression does not mean that every indicator deteriorates at once. Japan can record rising nominal GDP, exporter profits, positive real-wage months, functioning JGB auctions, and a stable banking system while the set of low-cost policy combinations narrows.

The practical test is compatibility: can authorities improve several binding constraints together without placing an equivalent public or private liability somewhere else?

<div class="formula-card">
  \[
  \mathcal{P}_t = \{p: x_i(p,t) \leq \bar{x}_i \text{ for every binding constraint } i\}
  \]
  <p><strong>Compression:</strong> fewer policy combinations remain inside every economic, institutional, and political tolerance.</p>
</div>

## Four transmission rules

| Rule | Operational meaning | Japanese example | Diagnostic question |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pressure valve** | One variable moves because authorities stabilize another | Yen depreciation under restrained domestic rates and managed long yields | Which price is moving because another is being controlled? |
| **Stress transfer** | Policy changes the payer or timing without removing the real cost | Energy support moves an import shock from household bills to the budget | Was the exposure reduced, or merely reassigned? |
| **Solution collision** | A tool improves its target while weakening the capacity needed to sustain it | A BOJ hike supports credibility but raises refinancing and credit costs | Does the secondary effect consume the policy's own operating room? |
| **Timeline pull-forward** | A shock turns a distant stock problem into a nearer flow problem | Higher marginal JGB yields enter debt service as legacy bonds mature | Which lag is shortening, and what must be financed sooner? |

<div class="flow-strip" aria-label="Pressure transfer sequence">
  <span>Price control</span><i>→</i><span>Pressure valve</span><i>→</i><span>Compensation</span><i>→</i><span>Collision</span><i>→</i><span>Narrower corridor</span>
</div>

No single link establishes fiscal dominance. Repetition across independent episodes does.

## The eight boxes

| Constraint box | Binding variable | Existing stabilizer | Secondary cost when heavily used |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fiscal** | Debt service, primary balance, shock capacity | Long maturities, tax revenue, domestic funding, asset income | Higher future interest cost and less room for shocks |
| **Monetary** | Inflation expectations, real rates, credibility | Policy rate, communication, JGB operations | Fiscal repricing, credit stress, or renewed market distortion |
| **JGB / financial** | Term premium, auction demand, duration absorption | BOJ holdings, banks, insurers, issuance management | Currency pressure, valuation loss, or impaired price discovery |
| **Foreign exchange** | Yen valuation and pass-through | Rate expectations, intervention, reserve assets | Reserve use, domestic tightening, or higher import inflation |
| **Household** | Real income, essential spending, consumption | Wages, savings, transfers, tax relief | Lower saving, weaker demand, or larger fiscal claims |
| **Corporate / SME** | Margins, refinancing, labor retention, investment | Cash, pricing power, credit, guarantees | Inflation, consolidation, employment loss, or bank credit costs |
| **Demographic / energy** | Labor capacity, dependency, import exposure | Participation, migration, automation, diversification | Transition spending and execution bottlenecks |
| **Political** | Tolerance for concentrated loss | Compensation, delay, coalition management | Recurring expenditure and weaker policy credibility |

## When the system becomes compressive

Three conditions must coincide:

1. more than one box is near its political or institutional tolerance;
2. the available remedy in one box worsens a variable already binding elsewhere; and
3. the buffers absorbing the collision replenish more slowly than they are used.

This distinction keeps the framework away from crisis theater. A weak auction, a falling yen, one bank failure, or a supplementary budget can matter without proving a national rupture. The evidence becomes regime-level when several markets and balance sheets begin forcing the same sequence.

## Compression versus decompression

<div class="signal-grid">
  <article class="signal-compression">
    <span>Compression signature</span>
    <ul>
      <li>Rate hikes coincide with faster fiscal and credit repricing.</li>
      <li>Yen intervention produces progressively shorter relief.</li>
      <li>Fiscal compensation expands after each price shock.</li>
      <li>JGB operations increasingly conflict with the tightening signal.</li>
      <li>Aggregate strength coexists with a widening vulnerable tail.</li>
    </ul>
  </article>
  <article class="signal-release">
    <span>Decompression signature</span>
    <ul>
      <li>Broad real wages lead durable private consumption.</li>
      <li>Productivity pays for wage growth across SMEs.</li>
      <li>Supplementary budgets return to exceptional use.</li>
      <li>Private JGB demand strengthens as BOJ support recedes.</li>
      <li>Several constraints ease without a new hidden liability.</li>
    </ul>
  </article>
</div>

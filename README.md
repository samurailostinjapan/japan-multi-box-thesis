# Japan’s Slow Compression

## Fiscal Dominance, Policy Collision, and the Transition from Managed Stability to Market-Driven Constraint

**Format:** Independent research monograph  
**Author:**  
**Evidence cutoff:** July 31, 2026  
**Target length:** 75–100 pages

Japan is not approaching a conventional sovereign default or a single dramatic collapse. It is entering a regime in which fiscal, monetary, demographic, household, corporate, energy, financial, and political constraints are tightening at the same time. The stabilizers that once allowed adjustment to be deferred remain substantial, but they are losing the capacity to insulate the entire system. As a result, policies that relieve one pressure increasingly intensify another.

This monograph develops and tests that argument through fiscal arithmetic, institutional analysis, market evidence, domestic transmission, demographic change, and structured scenarios. Facts, mechanism-based inferences, and stress estimates are identified separately. Official projections are treated as conditional models rather than forecasts.

## Editorial conventions

- Markdown files are self-contained and suitable for GitHub rendering.
- Citations use GitHub-compatible footnotes with direct source links.
- Japanese fiscal years are written as FY2026, FY2027, and so forth.
- Yen amounts use `¥` and `T` only in tables; prose uses “trillion yen.”
- “Debt service” means budgeted interest payments plus redemption transfers unless the text explicitly says “interest expenditure.”
- “Gross issuance” includes refunding bonds and is not interchangeable with new borrowing.
- Forecasts and stress paths are labeled; they are not presented as observed facts.
- “Slow compression” describes narrowing policy freedom and rising distributional costs. It does not imply imminent insolvency.
